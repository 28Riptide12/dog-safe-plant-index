# Plant Care Guide

Comprehensive guidance on caring for the plants in your garden to ensure they thrive alongside your dog.

## 🌱 General Plant Care Principles

### The Five Basics
1. **Water**: Proper hydration
2. **Light**: Right amount of sun/shade
3. **Soil**: Suitable growing medium
4. **Nutrients**: Feeding/fertilizing
5. **Space**: Room to grow

## 💧 Watering

### How Much Water?
Different plants need different amounts. The app categorizes plants by water needs:

#### Low Water (Drought Tolerant)
- Water every 14-21 days
- Let soil dry between waterings
- Examples: Grasses, Sedums, Rosemary
- Good for: Busy gardeners, sandy soil

#### Medium Water
- Water every 7-10 days
- Keep soil consistently moist
- Examples: Most perennials, Roses, Herbs
- Standard most common plants

#### High Water (Moisture Loving)
- Water every 2-5 days
- Keep soil wet but not waterlogged
- Examples: Hostas, Astilbe, Ferns
- Need: Rich soil, mulching

### Watering Tips
- **Check soil**: Stick finger 2 inches into soil
  - Dry? Water needed
  - Moist? Wait a few days
  - Wet? Don't water yet
- **Water early**: Morning watering reduces disease
- **Water roots**: Direct water to soil, not leaves
- **Consistency**: Regular schedule better than sporadic
- **Season matters**: Less in winter, more in summer
- **Watch for**: 
  - Wilting = needs water
  - Yellow leaves = too much water
  - Brown edges = inconsistent watering

### Weather Adjustments
- **After rain**: Skip watering (plants got rainfall)
- **Hot spell**: May need daily watering
- **Winter**: Much less water needed
- **Spring/Fall**: Gradually adjust frequency

## ☀️ Light Requirements

### Understanding Light Levels

#### Full Sun (6+ hours)
- Needs direct sunlight most of day
- South/west facing locations
- Examples: Roses, Lavender, Sedums
- Too much shade: Won't flower, becomes leggy

#### Partial Shade (3-6 hours)
- Needs some sun, tolerate shade
- North/east facing locations
- Afternoon shade in hot climates
- Examples: Astilbe, Coral Bells, some Ferns
- Flexible: Grow in varied conditions

#### Shade (Less than 3 hours)
- Prefer shaded locations
- North-facing walls, under trees
- Works under deciduous trees (leaf-on in summer)
- Examples: Ferns, Hostas, Ajuga
- Can burn in full sun

#### Dappled Shade
- Filtered sunlight through trees
- Best of both worlds
- Grows in understory plants
- Examples: Epimedium, Hellebores

### Tips
- Observe your garden through the day
- Morning sun in frost-prone areas (helps dry frost)
- Afternoon shade in hot climates (prevents scorch)
- South-facing warmer (north-facing cooler)
- Move tender plants to shade in extreme heat

## 🌍 Soil Types & Amendments

### Soil Types Explained

#### Clay Soil
- Heavy, dense, water-retaining
- Slow drainage, gets waterlogged
- Hard to dig, compacts easily
- **Improves with**: Grit, compost, organic matter
- **Plants for clay**: Astilbe, Hemerocallis, Hosta

#### Sandy Soil
- Light, drains quickly
- Dries out fast, loses nutrients
- Easy to dig and work
- **Improves with**: Compost, peat, manure
- **Plants for sand**: Lavender, Eryngium, Santolina

#### Loamy Soil
- Perfect balance of clay and sand
- Drains well, holds moisture
- Nutrient-rich, easy to work
- **Improves with**: Regular compost additions
- **Plants for loam**: Most plants thrive!

#### Mixed/Unknown Soil
- Varies across garden
- Test different areas
- Add compost universally (helps all soils)
- **Safe bet**: Add 2-3 inches compost annually

### Soil Amendments
- **Compost**: Improves structure, adds nutrients
- **Manure**: Adds nutrients, improves texture
- **Grit/Sand**: Improves drainage in heavy soil
- **Peat/Coir**: Improves moisture retention
- **Lime**: Raises pH for acid-sensitive plants
- **Sulphur**: Lowers pH for acid-loving plants

### When to Amend
- **Spring**: Add fresh compost (before growing season)
- **Fall**: Add manure (breaks down over winter)
- **Anytime**: Compost is always beneficial
- **Before planting**: Mix in amendments with soil

## 🍽️ Feeding & Fertilizing

### Fertilizer Types

#### Nitrogen (N)
- Promotes leafy green growth
- Use: Early spring, for foliage plants
- Too much: Lots of leaves, few flowers
- Good for: Hedges, hostas, ferns

#### Phosphorus (P)
- Promotes flowers and roots
- Use: Flower time (spring/early summer)
- Helps: Stronger root system
- Good for: Roses, perennials, bulbs

#### Potassium (K)
- Promotes overall health and hardiness
- Use: Fall to harden plants for winter
- Helps: Disease resistance, stress tolerance
- Good for: All plants

#### Balanced (N-P-K, e.g., 10-10-10)
- General purpose fertilizer
- Use: Spring and summer
- Works for: Most plants
- Safe bet: Use this if unsure

### Fertilizing Schedule
- **Spring**: Apply fertilizer as growth starts
- **Summer**: Feed monthly during growing season
- **Early Fall**: Final feeding to harden plants
- **Winter**: No feeding (dormant season)
- **Exception**: Continuous-flowering plants may need more

### Application Methods
- **Granular**: Mix into soil, water in
- **Liquid**: Dilute in watering can, apply to roots
- **Foliar**: Spray on leaves (fast absorption)
- **Slow-release**: Apply once, feeds all season

### Signs of Problems
- **Pale/yellow**: Nitrogen deficiency (feed)
- **Poor flowers**: Phosphorus deficiency (flower fertilizer)
- **Weak/stressed**: Potassium deficiency (balanced feed)
- **Overgrown, weak**: Too much nitrogen (reduce)
- **Burn marks**: Over-fertilizing (water well, skip feeding)

## ✂️ Pruning & Maintenance

### Why Prune?
- Removes dead/diseased wood
- Controls size and shape
- Encourages flowering
- Improves air circulation
- Keeps plants healthy

### Pruning Timing

#### Spring Pruning
- Remove winter damage
- Cut back dead stems
- Prune spring-flowering shrubs after flowering
- Cut back perennials
- Timing: When new growth starts

#### Summer Pruning
- Deadheading (remove spent flowers)
- Cut back faded flowers to encourage more
- Summer-trim hedges
- Remove crossing branches

#### Fall Pruning
- Light tidying only
- Avoid heavy pruning (stimulates tender new growth)
- Remove dead leaves
- Clean up fallen foliage

#### Winter Pruning
- Some dormant-season pruning okay
- Avoid pruning tender plants (wait for spring)
- Service pruning tools in winter
- Plan spring pruning now

### Deadheading (Removing Spent Flowers)
- Removes faded flowers
- Encourages more blooms
- Prevents seed-setting
- Frequency: 2-3 times weekly during bloom
- How: Pinch off flowers or use pruners
- Result: Longer flowering season

### Cutting Back Perennials
- Cut herbaceous perennials to ground in spring
- Timing: After frost danger passes, before growth
- Height: 1-2 inches above ground
- Result: Fresh new growth, healthier plant
- Examples: Daylilies, Coneflowers, Ornamental Grasses

### Tools Needed
- **Hand pruners**: For stems up to ¼ inch
- **Loppers**: For branches ¼ - ¾ inch
- **Pruning saw**: For larger branches
- **Hedge shears**: For trimming hedges
- **Maintenance**: Keep tools sharp, clean between cuts

### Spring
- [ ] Remove winter mulch (as new growth starts)

- [ ] Divide overgrown plants
- [ ] Apply spring fertilizer
- [ ] Edge beds and borders
- [ ] Stake tall plants early
- [ ] Watch for pest emergence

### Summer
- [ ] Water regularly (especially new plantings)
- [ ] Deadhead faded flowers
- [ ] Monitor for pests and diseases
- [ ] Fertilize monthly if needed
- [ ] Groom plants as needed
- [ ] Watch for heat stress
- [ ] Mulch to conserve moisture

- [ ] Stop fertilizing
- [ ] Reduce watering as growth slows
- [ ] Collect fallen leaves (for compost)

- [ ] Monitor for snow damage

- [ ] Review what worked/didn't work
- [ ] Order seeds/plants for spring
- [ ] Rest and enjoy garden design

## 🐛 Common Problems & Solutions

### Pest Issues
- **Aphids**: Small bugs, sticky honeydew
  - Solution: Strong spray of water, insecticidal soap
- **Slugs**: Holes in leaves, silvery trails
  - Solution: Beer traps, copper barriers, hand-pick
- **Spider mites**: Tiny webs, speckled leaves
  - Solution: Increase humidity, spray with water
- **Powdery mildew**: White powder on leaves
  - Solution: Remove infected leaves, improve air flow

### Disease Prevention
- Plant in appropriate conditions
- Don't overwater (reduces fungal issues)
- Space plants for air circulation
- Remove diseased foliage promptly
- Disinfect tools between cuts
- Avoid working on wet plants

### Nutrient Deficiencies
- **Pale yellow leaves**: Nitrogen needed
- **Poor flowering**: Phosphorus needed
- **Weak growth**: Potassium needed
- **Purple leaves**: Phosphorus deficiency
- **Solution**: Apply appropriate fertilizer

## 💚 Dog-Friendly Practices

### Safety Considerations
- Remove dead flowers/seed pods (could cause choking)
- Don't use toxic pesticides or herbicides
- Avoid cocoa mulch (toxic to dogs)
- Use dog-safe fertilizers
- Keep garden free of toxic plants
- Supervise dog access to garden

### Dog-Proof Gardening
- Protect tender plants from trampling
- Use barriers for prized plants
- Choose sturdy, hardy plants
- Avoid thorny plants at dog height
- Create dog-friendly areas with hardy grasses
- Check for sharp plant debris

### Non-Toxic Solutions
- Use neem oil for pests (not traditional pesticides)
- Spray water for aphids
- Hand-pick slugs and caterpillars
- Use beneficial insects
- Avoid toxic fungicides
- Choose organic methods
