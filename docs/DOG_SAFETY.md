# Dog Safety Guide

How to keep your dog safe in a garden filled with plants. All plants in this catalogue are non-toxic, but understanding plant safety is important.

## 🐕 About Dog-Safe Plants

### What "Non-Toxic" Means
Plants in this catalogue are:
- ✅ **Non-toxic to dogs** - Safe to be around
- ✅ **ASPCA verified** - Checked against official database
- ✅ **Safe if ingested in normal amounts** - Your dog won't be harmed from casual nibbling

### Important Caveats
- 🐕 Individual dogs may react differently
- 🐕 Some dogs may have allergies
- 🐕 Large quantities of ANY plant can cause upset stomach
- 🐕 Toxicity depends on plant part (leaf, seed, root, flower)
- 🐕 Young puppies are more vulnerable
- 🐕 Ingestion should be avoided whenever possible

## ⚠️ Plant Safety Levels

### Non-Toxic to Dogs (Safe)
✅ **Green Badge**
- Completely safe for dogs
- Can be in areas where dog has access
- Dog won't be harmed by casual nibbling
- Still supervise (normal hygiene practice)
- Examples: Roses, Marigolds, Lavender

### May Be Toxic (Caution)
⚠️ **Yellow Badge**
- Some risk if eaten in large quantities
- Plant parts vary in toxicity
- Safe if dog doesn't eat significant amounts
- Remove fallen leaves/seeds regularly
- Supervise dog access
- Examples: Tomato plants (leaves, stems toxic), Apple trees (seeds)

### Toxic (Avoid)
❌ **Red Badge**
- Toxic even in small quantities
- Can harm dogs significantly
- Should not be in dog gardens
- Keep in areas dog can't access
- Examples: Lilies, Daffodils, Sago Palm

## 🌿 Understanding Plant Toxicity

### What Part Is Toxic?
Different plant parts have different toxicity levels:

#### Leaves
- Most common toxic part
- Example: Tomato plant leaves are toxic, fruit is safe
- Risk: Dog nibbling/eating foliage

#### Seeds/Pods
- Often more concentrated toxins
- Example: Apple seeds, laburnum pods
- Risk: Attractive to dogs, higher toxicity

#### Roots/Bulbs
- Sometimes toxic when dug up
- Example: Daffodil bulbs highly toxic
- Risk: Dog digging garden

#### Flowers
- Usually safest part
- Example: Rose flowers safe (thorns uncomfortable)
- Risk: Generally low if non-toxic plant

#### Sap/Juices
- Can cause skin irritation
- Example: Fig sap can irritate skin
- Risk: Skin contact, ingestion

### How Toxins Work
- **Toxicity = dose-dependent**: Tiny amount OK, large amount harmful
- **Individual sensitivity**: Some dogs more sensitive than others
- **Size matters**: Toxin dose per pound body weight
  - Small dog: Lower threshold
  - Large dog: Higher tolerance
  - Still best to prevent ingestion

## 🚨 Toxic Plants to AVOID

### Common Toxic Plants

#### Bulbs (Highly Toxic!)
- ❌ Daffodils / Narcissus
- ❌ Lilies (especially Easter/Asiatic)
- ❌ Tulips
- ❌ Gladiolus
- ❌ Crocus
- Toxin: Glycosides in bulbs
- Symptoms: Vomiting, diarrhea, drooling, abdominal pain

#### Perennials (Often Toxic)
- ❌ Digitalis (Foxglove)
- ❌ Helleborus (Christmas Rose)
- ❌ Colchicum (Autumn Crocus)
- ❌ Anemone
- ❌ Ranunculus
- Toxin: Alkaloids, glycosides
- Symptoms: Cardiac issues, vomiting, diarrhea

#### Shrubs (Variable Toxicity)
- ❌ Rhododendron / Azalea
- ❌ Sago Palm
- ❌ Yew
- ❌ Oleander
- ❌ Laburnum (Golden Chain Tree)
- Toxin: Grayanotoxins, cardenolides
- Symptoms: Severe: cardiac, neurological

#### Trees (Avoid in Dog Gardens)
- ❌ Yew trees
- ❌ Laburnum
- ❌ Cherry laurel
- ❌ Apple (seeds only)
- ❌ Sago palm
- Toxin: Varies by species
- Symptoms: Varies; some very serious

#### House Plants (Not in Catalogue)
- ❌ Lilies (not dog-safe)
- ❌ Philodendron
- ❌ Sago Palm
- ❌ Oleander
- ❌ Autumn Crocus
- Never bring these toxic plants into homes with dogs

## 🐕 Dog Behavior & Garden Safety

### Why Dogs Eat Plants

#### Curiosity
- Young dogs explore with mouths
- Puppies chew on everything
- New plants smell interesting
- Solution: Supervise, redirect to appropriate toys

#### Boredom
- Dogs play with plants when bored
- May chew or dig
- Creates behavior and toxicity issues
- Solution: Provide toys, exercise, enrichment

#### Stomach Issues
- Dogs eat grass when feeling nauseous
- Instinctively seek plants
- Can indicate underlying problems
- Solution: Watch for patterns, consult vet

#### Nutritional Deficiency
- Rarely, indicates missing nutrients
- Dogs seek certain plants for minerals
- Usually not the primary cause
- Solution: Ensure balanced diet

#### Medical Issues
- Some medical conditions increase plant eating
- Diabetes, thyroid issues, etc.
- Not normal behavior if sudden
- Solution: Consult vet if behavior changes

### Prevention Strategies

#### Supervision
- **Watch your dog**: Know what they're doing
- **Especially with puppies**: Higher ingestion risk
- **In new gardens**: First few visits critical
- **After diet changes**: May affect behavior
- **When dogs are unsupervised**: Lock gate, barriers

#### Training
- **"Leave it" command**: Teach dog not to eat plants
- **Redirect**: Move dog to approved toys
- **Reward**: Praise when dog ignores plants
- **Consistency**: All family members enforce
- **Reinforcement**: Practice regularly

#### Environmental Management
- **Barriers**: Fence off plant areas
- **Remove temptations**: Dead leaves, fallen fruit
- **Tall plants**: Beyond jump reach for small dogs
- **Low plants**: Protect from digging
- **Alternative areas**: Gravel runs, dog-friendly zones

#### Garden Design
- **Dog zones**: Hardy grasses, cleared areas
- **Plant areas**: Fenced or separated
- **Pathways**: Keep clear of dropped material
- **Seating areas**: Away from toxic plants
- **Water access**: Separate from plant beds

## 🆘 What To Do If Dog Eats a Plant

### Immediate Steps

#### 1. **Identify the Plant**
- Take photo of plant if possible
- Note time of ingestion
- Estimate amount eaten
- Identify plant part (leaf, seed, flower, root)

#### 2. **Assess Symptoms**
Look for signs of poisoning:
- Vomiting or retching
- Diarrhea
- Drooling excessively
- Abdominal pain (arching, guarding)
- Lethargy/weakness
- Tremors/seizures
- Breathing difficulty
- Disorientation

#### 3. **Contact Immediately**
- **Local vet**: Call immediately
- **Emergency vet**: If after hours
- **Poison control**: Detailed guidance
  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: 1-888-426-4435 (call/text)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: 1-855-764-7661

#### 4. **Provide Information**
- Dog's weight and breed
- Plant name and part eaten
- Amount consumed (estimate)
- Time since ingestion
- Current symptoms

### Veterinary Care

#### What the Vet Will Do
- Physical examination
- Ask detailed questions
- May induce vomiting (if recent)
- May give activated charcoal
- IV fluids if needed
- Monitoring/treatment

#### Factors Affecting Severity
- Type of plant (toxicity level)
- Amount eaten (dose)
- Dog's size (smaller = higher risk)
- Time elapsed (faster treatment = better)
- Dog's health status (immunocompromised at higher risk)

#### Prognosis
- Most non-toxic plant ingestions: No serious outcome
- Minor toxins: Upset stomach, self-limiting
- Serious toxins: Can be life-threatening
- Early treatment: Usually improves outcomes
- Prevention: Always best approach

## 🏡 Dog-Friendly Garden Layout

### Zoning Strategy

#### Plant Beds (Monitored Area)
- Plants you want to keep
- Requires supervision
- Use dog-safe plants (from catalogue)
- Remove dropped leaves/seeds regularly
- Consider barriers during high-risk season

#### Dog Zone (Free Access)
- Hardwearing surfaces: Grass, mulch, gravel
- No plants or safe plants only
- Easy to clean
- Durable to dog traffic
- Toys and enrichment here

#### Seating Area (Mixed)
- Where you relax
- Can have non-dog-safe plants (with care)
- Supervision of nearby dog
- Clear view of other areas
- Monitor dropped material

#### Enclosed Area (Super Safe)
- Fenced off from main garden
- Dog has supervised access only
- All plants are 100% safe
- No toxins anywhere
- Complete freedom for dog

### Plant Choices for Dog Areas

#### Safe Ground Covers
- Dog-safe ornamental grasses
- Non-toxic clover
- Safe herbs (culinary)
- Non-thorny plants

#### Durable Plants
- Need to withstand dog traffic
- Hard to dig up
- Can't be crushed
- Recover quickly from damage
- Examples: Tough ornamental grasses, durable perennials

#### Avoid in Dog Areas
- Fragile plants (easily damaged)
- Thorny plants (uncomfortable)
- Toxic plants (obviously)
- Digging-prone plants (soft soil, easily uprooted)
- Tempting plants (attractive to chew)

## 🌿 Common Questions

### Q: Can my dog ever be in the garden with toxic plants?
**A**: Yes, with proper management:
- Fenced off or behind barriers
- Regular supervision
- Remove fallen material
- Dog training (leave it command)
- Good preventative measures

### Q: Are herbs safe for dogs?
**A**: Most culinary herbs in this catalogue are safe:
- ✅ Rosemary, Thyme, Sage, Oregano - All safe
- ✅ Parsley, Basil, Mint - Safe
- ⚠️ Some in small amounts
- Always verify before planting

### Q: What about tomato plants?
**A**: Tomato plant toxicity:
- ❌ Stems, leaves, unripe fruits - Toxic
- ✅ Ripe tomato fruits - Safe
- Solution: Protect plant from dog, allow ripe fruit only
- Alternative: Grow in raised bed dog can't access

### Q: Is it safe for dogs to eat grass?
**A**: Yes, generally safe:
- Normal dog behavior
- Dog-safe ornamental grasses OK
- Some grass eating normal
- Check it's pesticide-free
- Occasional vomiting normal
- Continuous vomiting = vet check

### Q: What about cocoa mulch?
**A**: **Avoid cocoa mulch completely!**
- Contains theobromine (chocolate toxin)
- Can poison dogs
- Chocolate smell attractive to dogs
- Use bark mulch instead
- Options: Wood chips, gravel, compost

### Q: Can my dog have a garden bed to dig in?
**A**: Yes, if managed safely:
- Create designated dig area
- Soft soil (sand/mulch mix)
- Supervise initially
- No toxic plants nearby
- Dog learns boundary
- Great enrichment

### Q: What if my dog has allergies?
**A**: Take extra precautions:
- Some dogs allergic to safe plants
- Watch for skin reactions
- After eating certain plants
- May have histamine sensitivities
- Consult vet if concerned
- May need restricted garden access

### Q: How do I train my dog not to eat plants?
**A**: Plant safety training:
1. Teach "Leave it" command
2. Practice around plants
3. Reward good behavior heavily
4. Redirect to toys
5. Supervise initially
6. Consistency essential
7. Professional trainer can help

### Q: What about pesticides and fertilizers?
**A**: Protect your dog:
- Many pesticides toxic to dogs
- Fertilizers can cause harm
- Use dog-safe products
- Organic/natural options available
- Apply when dog away
- Let dry completely
- Read labels carefully
- Wash paws after garden time
